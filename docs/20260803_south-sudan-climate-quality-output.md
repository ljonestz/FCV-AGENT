## Executive readout

The South Sudan Natural Resources & Livelihoods Project (P511185, US$59 million, concept note dated 15 June 2026) targets fishing communities, IDPs, refugees, and host populations across the Sudd ecosystem, Boma-Badingilo landscape, and Imatong Valley. At concept stage, the design already incorporates a meaningful set of climate-FCV responses that deserve recognition before gaps are assessed.

On climate integration, Sub-component 1.2 explicitly mandates climate-resilient, all-weather infrastructure standards accounting for seasonal flood rises exceeding 1.5 meters (PF-027). The ESMF commits to flood-resilient and drought-resilient practices (PF-024). CFM plans under Sub-component 2.3 are designed to be climate-aware, drawing on REDD+ frameworks and ESA monitoring data for carbon sequestration and ecosystem-based adaptation (PF-058, PF-059). The project acknowledges the dual nature of projected Sudd flooding—simultaneously boosting fish productivity and displacing communities—and sequences BFMU establishment before infrastructure becomes operational as a governance safeguard (PF-030, PF-051).

On FCV sensitivity, the design embeds several mandatory preconditions: participatory boundary delineation and land tenure verification before CWC investments (PF-052); site-specific ESMPs before civil works (PF-050); and Forest Management Plans before NTFP value chain activation (PF-049). A Security Risk Management Plan and SEP map conflict and elite capture risks per site (PF-044). Mandatory 40% female membership quotas in BFMUs, CWCs, and CFAs, combined with a GBV/SEAH Action Plan and dedicated PIU GBV Specialist, address exclusion risks (PF-043). The shared PIU leverages existing fiduciary and E&S capacity from SSRLSP (PF-020).

On responsiveness, the project pursues cooperative governance through an inter-ministerial NRM Working Group and PSC spanning MLF, MWCT, and MoEF (PF-021). Legislative reform of the 1954 Fisheries Act is supported (PF-057). PDO indicators track beneficiaries with enhanced climate resilience disaggregated by female, refugee, and host community status, and CBNRM organizations established with female-leadership tracking (PF-054, PF-055).

Four issues meet the threshold for previewing because each has a credited existing response and a defined residual gap.

First, BFMU continuity under active conflict. The project sequences BFMUs before infrastructure (ER-002) but no project fact specifies how co-management operations would be maintained or suspended during security deterioration in Sudd fisheries zones (RG-001). This is not yet specified, not an inherent design flaw; continuity protocols under insecurity remain undocumented at concept stage.

Second, climate-shock adaptive management triggers. The ESMF commits to flood-resilient and drought-resilient practices (ER-001), and the MEL framework tracks climate resilience beneficiaries (ER-008), but no project fact establishes early-warning thresholds or contingency triggers that would activate adaptive responses within implementation arrangements when flood pulse shifts or drought onset occur (RG-003). This is a specification gap, not an absence of intent.

Third, GBV referral pathway functionality at remote sites. The ESCP documents referral pathways and a survivor-centered GBV/SEAH Action Plan with a dedicated PIU specialist (ER-004), but no project fact confirms service availability or functional referral infrastructure at CWC sites in Boma-Badingilo or the Imatong CFM pilot (RG-006). Remote conflict-affected geography makes this a material operationalization question.

Fourth, the PDO habitat hectares indicator lacks a target value (PF-053, RG-005). The indicator exists and is credited (ER-008), but without a baseline and target, it cannot confirm whether ecosystem-based climate adaptation outcomes are being achieved. This is explicitly flagged as not yet specified.

Two items are noted but do not yet meet the admission threshold: transboundary hydrological risk screening (RG-008) rests on low-confidence evidence and is not established as a project gap; instrument type resolution (RG-004) is a preparation-stage administrative matter without demonstrated climate-FCV consequence at this stage.

Summary of four judgments: Relevance is high—South Sudan's second-place INFORM ranking, six major floods and two droughts in fifteen years, and the project's direct targeting of climate-exposed fisheries and forest communities establish material Climate-FCV intersection. Sensitivity is moderate—the design recognizes core FCV dynamics and embeds meaningful preconditions and safeguards, but BFMU conflict-continuity protocols and site-specific conflict assessments remain unspecified. Responsiveness is emerging—cooperative governance structures, legislative reform support, ecosystem-based adaptation, and disaggregated resilience indicators are present, but adaptive management triggers and verified referral infrastructure are not yet specified. Operationalization is partial—decision gates, mandatory preconditions, and indicator frameworks are documented, but target values, enforcement capacity specifications, and contingency protocols remain to be defined in subsequent preparation and implementation instruments. No overall rating is produced.

## Review readiness flags for task-team verification

### RF-001 — material_placeholder

**Why it matters:** Instrument type affects conditionality design, disbursement triggers, and fragile-state implementation flexibility; unresolved status may delay appraisal and affect risk allocation.

### RF-002 — unresolved_indicator

**Flag:** PDO habitat hectares target value is recorded as 'xx hectares' with no baseline or methodology specified in the concept note.

**Why it matters:** Without a target and baseline, the primary ecosystem-based climate adaptation outcome indicator cannot confirm whether the PDO is being achieved or support results reporting.

**Document basis:** PF-053

**Suggested verification:** Task team to confirm whether a preparation-stage ecological baseline is planned and document the target-setting approach before appraisal submission.

### RF-003 — incomplete_climate_screening

**Flag:** No project fact confirms whether climate-driven IDP and refugee displacement concentration zones have been mapped against fisheries and forest project sites.

**Why it matters:** Unscreened population-resource pressure from displacement influx could undermine co-management governance and trigger resource conflict at project sites before investments are operational.

**Document basis:** PF-056, PF-017, PF-016

**Suggested verification:** Confirm with the task team whether the SEP or Security Risk Management Plan preparation includes displacement pressure mapping at each project site.

### RF-004 — missing_operational_home

**Flag:** GBV and SEAH referral pathways are documented in the ESCP, but no project fact confirms functional referral service availability at remote CWC and CFM sites.

**Why it matters:** Non-functional referral pathways at remote conflict-affected sites would render the GBV/SEAH Action Plan ineffective and expose survivors to unmitigated harm, creating reputational and compliance risk.

**Document basis:** PF-043, PF-018, PF-019

**Suggested verification:** Task team to confirm whether a service-mapping exercise for GBV referral infrastructure at CWC and CFM sites is planned during preparation or Year 1 implementation.
