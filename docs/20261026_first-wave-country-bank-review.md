Existing decision notes: This schema-1.1 Yemen candidate uses verified public sources, treats structural water scarcity, governance, conflict damage, economic collapse, food insecurity, and climate variability as distinct but interacting drivers, and avoids single-cause water-conflict claims. It requires Lindsey Jones's substantive review and is not approved for production. Territorial access, aquifer monitoring, localized projections, de facto authority coverage, independent long-term evaluation, and quantitative reverse-pathway evidence remain explicit gaps.

Evidence scope: YEM-E-001, YEM-E-002, YEM-E-003, YEM-E-004, YEM-E-005, YEM-E-006, YEM-E-007, YEM-E-008, YEM-E-009, YEM-E-010, YEM-E-011, YEM-E-012, YEM-E-013, YEM-E-014, YEM-E-015, YEM-E-016.

#### Current tier review priorities

- YEM-E-005: IPC projects 5.4 million people in Government-controlled areas in Phase 3 or worse during June-September 2026, while local fieldwork shows women, displaced people, and poorer farmers face distinct asset, labour, water, and mobility constraints.
  - Stored due date: 2026-11-10. Geography: Government-controlled areas; Abyan; Hadramawt; Marib; Taiz.
  - Uncertainty: IPC covers 118 districts in 12 governorates and is multi-causal; local livelihood evidence is purposive and not nationally representative.
  - Source passages: [YEM-SRC-007](https://www.ipcinfo.org/fileadmin/user_upload/ipcinfo/docs/IPC_Yemen_GoY_Acute_Food_Insecurity_Mar_Dec2026_Report.pdf): p. 2 and pp. 19-20, headline results, drivers, and evidence level; [YEM-SRC-013](https://sanaacenter.org/files/Livelihood_Resilience_and_Adaptation_Practices_Among_Agricultural_Communities_in_Yemen_en.pdf): pp. 31-35, social dynamics and marginalization
  - Review decision: pending.

- YEM-E-007: Farmers in six study communities reported crop switching, solar pumping, collective water-system maintenance, income diversification, and migration, but solar pumping also enabled deeper abstraction and excluded some poorer farmers.
  - Stored due date: 2026-11-10. Geography: six communities in Abyan, Hadramawt, Marib, and Taiz.
  - Uncertainty: Practices are self-reported in a purposive local sample and their durability was not evaluated.
  - Source passages: [YEM-SRC-013](https://sanaacenter.org/files/Livelihood_Resilience_and_Adaptation_Practices_Among_Agricultural_Communities_in_Yemen_en.pdf): pp. 21 and 25, water governance and adaptation typology
  - Review decision: pending.

- YEM-E-008: The NDC 3.0 Vision adopts an adaptation-first approach, names vulnerable groups, assigns sector goals and lead institutions, and sets a 2026-2035 roadmap, but does not establish that those arrangements are operational across divided institutions.
  - Stored due date: 2026-11-10. Geography: Yemen.
  - Uncertainty: The document is a Vision anticipating finalization, and implementation across territorially divided systems is unverified.
  - Source passages: [YEM-SRC-003](https://unfccc.int/sites/default/files/2025-11/Yemens%20NDC3.0%20Vision.pdf): pp. 2, 8, and 11-12, adaptation principles, Table 3, and finalization roadmap
  - Review decision: pending.

- YEM-E-009: Water governance is fragmented across national, basin, local, customary, and private actors, while conflict has weakened monitoring, enforcement, utility finance, and whole-catchment coordination.
  - Stored due date: 2026-11-10. Geography: Yemen; Taiz; selected basins.
  - Uncertainty: Capacity varies among basins and authorities, and current comparable performance data are incomplete.
  - Source passages: [YEM-SRC-002](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099111924095042411/pdf/P180386156c0320eb1913217409bcab5d7a.pdf): pp. 95-111, institutional, basin, monitoring, and enforcement findings
  - Review decision: pending.

- YEM-E-010: UNDP evaluations find that local institutions and delivery partners retained meaningful implementation reach, while territorial access, staffing, maintenance finance, inclusion, monitoring, and exit arrangements constrained sustainability and equitable performance.
  - Stored due date: 2026-11-10. Geography: seven evaluation governorates; nine governorates and 47 districts.
  - Uncertainty: Evaluations do not cover all authorities or areas and do not causally estimate climate-resilience outcomes.
  - Source passages: [YEM-SRC-008](https://erc.undp.org/evaluation/documents/download/24377): pp. 5-9 and 45-55, evaluation findings, geographic limits, and sustainability; [YEM-SRC-011](https://erc.undp.org/evaluation/documents/download/24918): pp. 4-10 and 70-83, findings, limitations, sustainability, and recommendations
  - Review decision: pending.

- YEM-E-012: Independent reviews of emergency public works and institutional resilience document delivery and employment outputs but find limited evidence on sustained welfare, maintenance, institutionalization, peace, or climate-resilience outcomes.
  - Stored due date: 2026-11-10. Geography: project areas across Yemen; nine governorates and 47 districts.
  - Uncertainty: Neither review provides a robust counterfactual for peace or climate-resilience outcomes.
  - Source passages: [YEM-SRC-010](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099082923164015722/pdf/P159053154192c08f1a9281213e9202e4c1.pdf): pp. 3-10 and 18-22, objectives, efficacy, monitoring, and lessons; [YEM-SRC-011](https://erc.undp.org/evaluation/documents/download/24918): pp. 4-10 and 70-83, results, limitations, sustainability, and recommendations
  - Review decision: pending.

- YEM-E-013: Local assessments identify disputes around wells, irrigation, upstream-downstream allocation, land, and floodwater, but climate and water scarcity operate through governance, ownership, livelihoods, authority, and existing conflict rather than as single causes of violence.
  - Stored due date: 2026-11-10. Geography: selected districts in Abyan, Hadramawt, and Dhamar; selected Yemen examples.
  - Uncertainty: Evidence is local, qualitative, and partly project-linked; it does not establish a national or direct climate-to-violence effect.
  - Source passages: [YEM-SRC-004](https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2024-06/Water-related%20Conflict%20Assessment%20Report_2024_1.pdf): pp. 9-10, 29-30, and local district assessments; [YEM-SRC-005](https://www.sipri.org/sites/default/files/2023-06/2023_sipri-nupi_fact_sheet_yemen_june.pdf): pp. 2-3, livelihood, mobility, and water-conflict pathways
  - Review decision: pending.

- YEM-E-014: Conflict damage, territorial fragmentation, economic collapse, fuel scarcity, and service disruption weaken water, energy, health, agriculture, monitoring, and aid systems needed to manage climate hazards.
  - Stored due date: 2026-11-10. Geography: Yemen; Sana'a; Taiz.
  - Uncertainty: The reverse mechanism is strongly documented, but its additional quantitative effect on climate losses is not estimated.
  - Source passages: [YEM-SRC-002](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099111924095042411/pdf/P180386156c0320eb1913217409bcab5d7a.pdf): pp. 3-8 and 95-111, conflict effects on services, monitoring, and governance; [YEM-SRC-012](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/12/11/3269): pp. 12-18, conflict-era infrastructure and provider adaptation
  - Review decision: pending.

- YEM-E-015: Water-user groups, customary mediation, community organizations, and local delivery institutions can support allocation, dispute management, and recovery when representation, legitimacy, financing, hydrological fit, and links to public authority are credible.
  - Stored due date: 2026-11-10. Geography: selected districts and programme areas.
  - Uncertainty: Potential and local delivery are documented, but durable climate-resilience and peace outcomes were not causally evaluated.
  - Source passages: [YEM-SRC-004](https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2024-06/Water-related%20Conflict%20Assessment%20Report_2024_1.pdf): pp. 9-10 and 29-30, water-user associations and mediation recommendations; [YEM-SRC-011](https://erc.undp.org/evaluation/documents/download/24918): pp. 49-58 and 70-83, local institutional results and sustainability
  - Review decision: pending.

## Production boundary

The loader changes are separate from country approval. Passing tests authorizes neither the evidence nor a production release. Promote only the signed-off version, pin its bank commit, then complete the agreed service and export checks. A partial release must exclude unresolved records and any unsupported pathway or profile content that depends on them.

The unchanged approved July South Sudan bank remains the rollback baseline. Broader collection and the browser research package follow completion of the existing-bank approval and production work.
