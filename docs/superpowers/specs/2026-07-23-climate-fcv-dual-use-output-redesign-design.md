# Climate-FCV Dual-Use Output Redesign

**Date:** 23 July 2026
**Status:** Approved design, pending implementation plan
**Branch:** `codex/climate-fcv-output-redesign`

## 1. Purpose

Refine the manually selected Climate-FCV lens as an integrated dual-use
assessment. A Climate-active run must retain a complete, credible core FCV
assessment while giving Climate-FCV analysis comparable analytical depth and
visibility. It must not read like a normal FCV report with a generic Climate
appendix.

This design deliberately preserves the option of adding a user-selectable
Climate-primary mode later. That future mode is out of scope. The current
implementation remains one integrated mode.

## 2. Fixed Product Decisions

- Climate remains manually selected and is never auto-suggested.
- Core-only behavior remains unchanged, including its research depth,
  four-to-five substantive priorities, and lightweight conditional
  Climate-FCV check.
- An active Climate lens supersedes the lightweight check.
- Climate-active runs retain the core FCV framework and ratings, but rebalance
  research, context, and output budgets so core and Climate-FCV analysis have
  comparable depth.
- Adaptation and resilience are primary. Mitigation or transition analysis
  requires a clear, material project pathway.
- Climate-active outputs contain no more than five substantive priorities.
  Their core, Climate, or blended composition remains evidence-led rather than
  quota-driven.
- Publicly available CCDR material is preferred where directly relevant but
  remains optional contextual support and must not dominate the analysis.
- Generic Climate-FCV or dividend claims are worse than an explicit evidence
  gap and must be suppressed.

## 3. Output Architecture

### 3.1 Climate materiality panel

Retain the existing blue Climate materiality panel. It should provide:

- a High, Medium, or Low judgment;
- a concise project-specific rationale;
- the principal locations, systems, groups, or assets that make the pathway
  material;
- a brief evidence limitation where relevant.

The panel is an orientation device, not a substitute for the analysis that
follows.

### 3.2 Integrated executive summary

Retain the existing core structure:

1. bold opening assessment;
2. operational context;
3. strengths;
4. gaps;
5. FCV sensitivity;
6. FCV responsiveness.

For Climate-active runs, Climate-FCV evidence must be meaningfully integrated
into each subsection where material. The opening assessment and operational
context must normally identify the most important Climate-FCV implication.
Strengths and gaps should address material Climate-FCV design features rather
than adding token references. Sensitivity and responsiveness should explain
how the project recognizes, manages, or responds to the diagnosed pathways.

The narrative must remain integrated. It must not create duplicate core and
Climate paragraphs or displace the wider FCV assessment.

### 3.3 Two substantive Climate-FCV interaction sections

Replace the current shallow interaction cards with two analytical subsections:

1. **How Climate-FCV dynamics could affect this project**
2. **How this project could influence Climate-FCV dynamics**

Each subsection must contain:

- a project- and location-specific causal narrative;
- named components, activities, institutions, assets, sites, or groups;
- positive and adverse pathways where supported;
- operational and outcome implications;
- current design responses and remaining gaps;
- evidence, confidence, and uncertainty;
- relevant time horizons.

Each narrative is followed by a compact causal strip:

> **Climate/FCV pressure → mediated mechanism → project implication → design response**

Small time-horizon badges may supplement the strip. Additional decorative
graphics are out of scope until the analytical content is consistently strong.

### 3.4 Qualitative Climate, peace and social dividends synthesis

Remove the six current dividend cards. Replace them with a short qualitative
section that explains:

- how the current project design already contributes to credible Climate,
  peace, and social dividends;
- which contributions are supported versus potential;
- the most material opportunities to maximize them;
- important risks, trade-offs, or maladaptation concerns;
- which numbered priorities carry the opportunities forward.

The six established pathway concepts remain available to the diagnostic, but
only credible and project-relevant pathways appear in the narrative. Empty
categories are suppressed.

### 3.5 Unified priorities

Retain a single ranked list of no more than five substantive priorities.
Purely core FCV priorities remain eligible where they outrank weaker Climate
candidates.

In Climate-active runs, replace the per-priority **Differentiated approach
note** with **Climate, peace and social dividend contribution**:

- where a material pathway exists, identify it and explain the project-specific
  mechanism and how the priority strengthens it;
- where none exists, state that no material dividend pathway was identified
  and explain why the priority was retained on core FCV grounds.

This panel appears on every priority in a Climate-active run. It must never
force a benefit claim.

The differentiated country-category framing remains near the beginning of the
report and continues to calibrate recommendations internally. Core-only
priority cards remain unchanged.

## 4. Research and Analytical Pipeline

### 4.1 Stage 1: shared research plan

When Climate is active, Stage 1 creates one shared research plan for the core
FCV and Climate-FCV passes. It extracts from the uploaded package:

- country and subnational project locations;
- components, activities, institutions, and long-lived assets;
- livelihood and natural-resource systems;
- affected and potentially excluded groups;
- implementation and governance arrangements.

The shared plan prevents duplicated searches for displacement, resource
governance, livelihoods, insecurity, or institutional capacity.

### 4.2 Dedicated Climate-FCV research pass

An active Climate lens triggers a dedicated research pass covering:

- observed and projected hazards and changing seasonal patterns;
- subnational impacts relevant to project locations;
- sector, livelihood, ecosystem, infrastructure, and service implications;
- displacement, mobility, food-security, resource-access, governance, and
  conflict pathways;
- differentiated exposure, voice, and adaptive capacity;
- project-induced resilience, vulnerability, emissions, transition,
  exclusion, maladaptation, and risk-transfer pathways;
- credible Climate, peace, and social dividend evidence.

The source hierarchy is:

1. a public country CCDR where one exists and contains directly relevant
   material;
2. other World Bank sources;
3. authoritative UN, scientific, government, and established specialist
   sources;
4. reputable current reporting only where needed to establish recent shocks
   or operational conditions.

CCDR retrieval is a targeted first check, not a requirement or a source quota.

### 4.3 Structured research claims

Stage 1 research is distilled before entering Stage 2. Each claim records:

- claim or finding;
- source identifier, title, URL, and date;
- geography;
- affected group, system, institution, or asset;
- relevance to a named project element;
- observed, projected, or inferred status;
- confidence and evidence gap;
- applicable time horizon.

Long raw research prose is not carried through later context windows.

### 4.4 Climate research failure

A timed-out Climate research pass receives one bounded retry with a narrower
query. If the retry fails:

- record a visible evidence limitation;
- retain the core FCV assessment;
- do not substitute generic Climate-FCV claims;
- suppress interaction or dividend content that cannot pass validation;
- keep the active Climate lens from triggering the lightweight core check.

## 5. Depth and Budget Rebalancing

Core-only budgets and behavior do not change.

For Climate-active runs:

- retain the full core FCV standards, ratings, and safeguards coverage;
- make core contextual research more selective and project-material;
- allocate comparable evidence and reasoning capacity to the Climate-FCV pass;
- avoid duplicating evidence across the two streams;
- condense the visible core Stage 2 narrative where needed without dropping
  mandatory analysis;
- carry only distilled claims and bounded diagnostic objects between stages;
- perform no new web research in Stage 3.

The objective is not an arbitrary 50/50 word split. It is comparable analytical
depth within stable time and context budgets.

## 6. Stage 2 Causal Diagnostic

Stage 2 combines project evidence and structured research claims. Every
material interaction must identify:

- Climate and FCV pressures;
- the mediated causal mechanism;
- a named project component, activity, location, group, institution, or asset;
- likely operational or outcome implications;
- current project response and remaining gap;
- evidence source and confidence;
- applicable time horizon;
- a practical design response where warranted.

### 6.1 Specificity gate

Suppress a claim unless it explains:

- which Climate or FCV pressure;
- where and for whom it matters;
- through which resource, institution, asset, service, or delivery mechanism;
- why it matters to this project.

Statements such as “Climate stress may intensify inter-community tensions” do
not pass without this causal and project-specific detail.

### 6.2 Time horizons

Each material pathway uses one or more of:

- **Current/near term:** preparation and early implementation conditions;
- **Project lifetime:** changing exposure, seasonality, institutions, and
  delivery conditions over roughly one to five years;
- **Asset/system lifetime:** changes over five to fifteen years or longer where
  financed assets, ecosystems, institutions, or livelihood systems outlast the
  financing period.

Long-lived investments must distinguish limited immediate disruption from
material implications for location, design standards, durability, governance,
maintenance, distribution, or maladaptation.

## 7. Structured Links and Rendering

Stage 2 assigns stable identifiers to:

- both interaction directions and their causal pathways;
- validated dividend pathways;
- material candidate findings.

Stage 3 priority JSON carries the relevant interaction and dividend identifiers
plus a Climate relevance explanation. The renderer derives Climate provenance,
pathway labels, and the priority contribution panel from these structured links.
It must not depend on the model remembering a free-text `lens_ids` tag.

New priority fields must be additive and backward-compatible with the existing
shared priority JSON contract. The implementation must record the contract
change in the private build-parity log so the companion build can mirror it.

This deterministic link resolves the observed case where High Climate
materiality produced Climate-relevant priorities but no Climate-tagged priority.

The same validated structures drive:

- the live results page;
- shared HTML;
- `.docx` export.

## 8. Validation and Safe Fallback

A Climate diagnostic is usable only when it passes:

1. **Structural validation:** valid materiality, both directions, horizons,
   sources, and identifiers;
2. **Specificity validation:** project element plus relevant location, group,
   institution, system, or asset;
3. **Evidence validation:** trusted source, project evidence, or explicit
   inference/evidence gap;
4. **Usefulness validation:** a causal pathway reaches a concrete project
   implication or design decision.

If the diagnostic remains invalid after bounded recovery:

- Stage 3 preserves normal core FCV behavior;
- no unvalidated Climate-specific readout, dividend, priority, or materiality
  claim is generated;
- the output explains the evidence or diagnostic limitation;
- the active Climate lens continues to supersede the lightweight check.

## 9. Testing

Automated coverage must include:

- unchanged core-only prompts, budgets, output, and conditional Climate check;
- balanced Climate-active prompt and research budgets;
- CCDR-present and CCDR-absent cases;
- Climate research timeout, bounded retry, and safe failure;
- structured research claim normalization;
- current, project-lifetime, and asset-lifetime pathways;
- rejection of generic or ungrounded interaction claims;
- valid positive and adverse project-influence pathways;
- maladaptation and distributional-risk examples;
- deterministic interaction and dividend links for every priority;
- High materiality with Climate-linked priorities;
- a valid no-material-dividend priority panel;
- suppression of weak dividend categories;
- parity across the live page, HTML, and `.docx`;
- an end-to-end South Sudan regression;
- at least one Low-materiality comparison case.

Safe production telemetry may record:

- timing and retry status;
- source counts and allowlisted source types;
- diagnostic structure and identifier coverage;
- specificity-gate failure counts;
- output and context sizes.

It must not log project prose, evidence text, prompts, credentials, or
untrusted arbitrary keys.

## 10. Out of Scope

- A user-selectable Integrated versus Climate-primary mode;
- automatic Climate lens suggestion or activation;
- changes to core-only analysis;
- a separate Climate score;
- a separate Climate priority list;
- mandatory CCDR dependence;
- decorative charts beyond causal strips, horizon badges, and pathway labels;
- deep mitigation analysis without a material project pathway.

## 11. Success Criteria

The redesign succeeds when a Climate-active output:

- remains recognizably a full FCV assessment;
- makes Climate-FCV material in the opening, core narrative, interactions, and
  priorities;
- uses country-, subnational-, and project-specific evidence;
- distinguishes current conditions from project- and asset-lifetime risks;
- provides two nuanced causal directions rather than generic lists;
- replaces dividend card proliferation with a useful qualitative synthesis;
- explains the Climate/dividend relevance of every priority without forcing
  benefits;
- completes reliably within bounded production time and context limits;
- degrades safely when research or diagnostics fail.
