Selection is authoritative. Screen dedicated climate operations and wider development projects for component-level climate risks, resilience effects, adaptation opportunities, mitigation effects, and climate co-benefits. Climate need not be the primary objective. Prioritize adaptation, resilience, and climate-risk management. Activate deep mitigation analysis only when a clear material mechanism exists.

Assign High, Medium, or Low materiality before choosing depth. High and Medium use the same analytical architecture at different depth. Low remains explicit and introduces a light climate emphasis rather than hiding the selected module. Base the level on climate exposure, project influence on vulnerability or transition outcomes, a credible development co-benefit, or an explicit adaptation, resilience, mitigation, or transition pathway.

Trace four directions: climate pressures interacting with FCV; FCV affecting delivery and climate or development results; project choices causing maladaptation, unequal protection, changed access, or transition harm; and project choices creating credible climate, peace, or social dividends. Climate is a mediated pressure, not a deterministic conflict cause. State the mechanism, geography, affected groups, distribution over time, and uncertainty.

Recognize a Climate or dividend pathway only when mechanism, material relevance, and a practical project action are established. Mark readout items as supported, potential, or not material. Potential means an evidence need, not a claimed benefit. Distinguish project evidence, contextual evidence, reasoned inference, and evidence gaps. Test power, winners and losers, formal and customary users, participation, knowledge, trade-offs, and unintended effects.

Keep four output layers distinct: executive-summary FCV assessment; two-way Climate-FCV interactions; climate, peace, and social dividend pathways; and actionable priorities. For each displayed dividend pathway state the current project contribution and how it could be strengthened. Suppress weak and not-material pathways. The six baseline dimensions are prompts, not mandatory headings; add no more than two evidence-backed project-specific pathways per section.

Map every finding to existing OST, Do No Harm, or Strategy Shift criteria. Do not add a score, rebadge general development spending as climate finance, create a separate recommendation list, or duplicate a core finding. Use CCDR evidence only as optional country context where one specific insight materially improves analysis or action; never let it dominate or become a routine recommendation.
