# Defueling Conflict - Notes from the Field

## Authority and role
World Bank field guidance on applying conflict sensitivity and environmental peacebuilding across upstream analytics, project design, safeguards, monitoring and learning, and institutional capacity building.

## Key propositions
- Upstream analysis should define an explicit theory of change connecting environmental pressures, institutions, users, conflict dynamics, and feasible project influence.
- Project design and safeguards should anticipate differentiated access, participation constraints, unintended effects, and delivery risks rather than relying on generic commitments.
- Monitoring, evaluation, and learning should combine outcome measures, perceptions, grievance information, field access, and early warning so teams can adapt.
- Institutional capacity building should address incentives, relationships, legitimacy, coordination, and practical functions, not training alone.

## Questions supported
Institutional feasibility, governance and trust, monitoring and adaptation, investment-side dividends, and operational delivery.

## Recommendation implications
Translate analysis into sequenced decisions, named responsibilities, feedback loops, and feasible indicators. Use adaptive monitoring to test both harm and peace-positive hypotheses.

## Caveats
Environmental peacebuilding outcome groups provide analytical possibilities, not a promise of peace. Tools must be calibrated to instrument, lifecycle, access, and capacity.
