# Maximizing the Peace and Social Dividends of Climate Action

## Authority and role
World Bank Group operational framing source. Barron, Jones, and El-Horr (2026) organize the positive opportunity side of the lens while retaining proportionality and harm checks.

## Key propositions
- FCV settings can face a triple burden of high climate vulnerability, low resilience, and weak peace and social dynamics.
- The operational framework separates what projects may invest in - cohesion and inclusion, institutional capacity and legitimacy, and socioeconomic opportunity - from how they may deliver through context analysis, trust and collaboration, and adaptive management.
- Dividends are prospective and context-dependent, not automatic labels. Wider development and humanitarian finance can contain genuine climate co-benefits without being rebadged as climate finance.
- Audit-only anchor: triple-burden proportionality test.

## Questions supported
Climate materiality, institutional feasibility, livelihoods, governance and trust, monitoring and adaptation, and both dividend readout sections.

## Recommendation implications
Foreground only project-specific pathways with a stated mechanism, material relevance, practical action, and monitoring approach. Check adverse effects and avoid filling all six categories.

## Caveats
Case-study insights do not prove applicability to a particular operation. Peace and social outcomes must not displace the project's primary climate or development purpose.
