# Conflict-Sensitive Climate Change Adaptation - A Review

## Authority and role
Nadiruzzaman, Scheffran, Shewly, and Kley (2022), Sustainability 14, 8060. Academic review of 35 peer-reviewed field studies on conflict-sensitive climate adaptation.

## Key propositions
- Inclusive and emancipatory participation by local and marginalized populations is central, while practical approaches differ across contexts.
- Adaptation politics can generate conflict in settings without prior violence; a narrow focus on overt conflict misses quieter exclusion and distributional harm.
- Local geography, traditional and situated knowledge, and the politics of whose knowledge counts affect adaptation choices and legitimacy.
- Costs and benefits should be assessed for fairness, and proposed adaptation should be pretested through a conflict lens.

## Questions supported
Differentiated effects, maladaptation, participation and grievance systems, and distribution and power.

## Recommendation implications
Test who defines risk, whose knowledge shapes design, who pays, who benefits, and whether participation changes decisions. Treat missing voices as an evidence gap rather than proof of failure.

## Caveats
The review notes geographic and evidentiary concentration in parts of Africa and Asia and reliance on generalized climate evidence where field data are unavailable.
