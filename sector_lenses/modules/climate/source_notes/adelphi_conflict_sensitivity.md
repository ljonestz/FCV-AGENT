# Addressing Climate-Related Security Risks

## Authority and role
adelphi and UNEP guidance (2022) for conflict-sensitive climate adaptation and sustainable-livelihood programming in fragile and conflict-affected settings.

## Key propositions
- Integrated analysis should examine conflict drivers, connectors, actor relationships, power, climate exposure, sensitivity, and adaptive capacity.
- Actor categories are internally diverse; analysis should identify affected groups and their relationships rather than treating refugees, displaced people, or communities as homogeneous.
- Best, worst, and most likely scenarios help teams explore uncertainty and future interactions among hazards, institutions, and conflict dynamics.
- Entry points should be assessed for conflict sensitivity and robustness across plausible scenarios before implementation.

## Questions supported
Compound risk, delivery feasibility, monitoring and adaptation, participation, and transboundary systems.

## Recommendation implications
Use actor and power analysis, integrated scenarios, locally informed entry points, and robustness checks. Monitor whether activities worsen tensions or create new exclusions.

## Caveats
The Nepal and Sudan experience illustrates methods rather than universally transferable interventions. Climate-security language must retain mediated causal reasoning.
