# Conflict-Sensitive Climate Action and the Root Drivers of Conflict

## Authority and role
CGIAR COP29 Issue Brief 8 (2024). It supplies a concise causal guardrail against simplistic threat-multiplier language and highlights institutional and political pathways.

## Key propositions
- Climate effects interact with social, political, economic, historical, and environmental factors; they do not directly or inevitably produce organized violence.
- Political interests, resources, incentives, and institutions help determine whether climate-related grievances escalate.
- Resource management, livelihood disruption, disasters, mobility, and transition policies can create risk pathways or peace-supporting opportunities.
- Accountable institutions, inclusive governance, and conflict-sensitive climate strategies are central to managing these interactions.

## Questions supported
Compound risk, disasters and services, mobility and displacement, and mitigation or transition pathways.

## Recommendation implications
Name mediating drivers and feasible project levers. Pair climate actions with governance, inclusion, and monitoring where the mechanism is material; never substitute a threat-multiplier label for analysis.

## Caveats
The four-page brief synthesizes a field rather than providing project-level evidence. A peace-multiplier possibility still requires a demonstrated project mechanism and safeguards.
